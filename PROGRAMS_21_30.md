## Assumptions and interpretation

- Ability-based stages are selected deliberately using the stated criteria; the app does not advance a beginner by date alone.
- Gentle mobility, conditioning and power practice are not counted as hypertrophy hard sets. Time and distance targets use their own tracking units and effort cues.
- The kettlebell block uses one suitable bell and no bench; park training requires secure suitable bars and steps.
- The low-impact and equipment-free fat-loss plans work in any location. They do not promise universal pain-free movement, spot reduction, or preservation of strength in tasks that are not trained.
- These original schedules are informed by general principles, not individually validated research protocols.

## 21. Push-Up Progression

Three weekly practice sessions build from wall and knee push-ups toward controlled full and advanced variations. Progress follows demonstrated technique rather than automatic weekly difficulty jumps.

**Equipment:** bodyweight.

**Ability stages:**
- **wall:** Start here until wall reps are controlled at the stated range and reserve. Omit eccentric-pushup. 
- **knee-eccentric:** Two sessions of 15 controlled wall reps per set and the ability to lower a knee or full push-up without hip sag. Omit none. Replace wall-pushup with Knee Push-up — 3 × 6–12, rest 90s, 3 RIR.
- **full:** Two sessions of 12 controlled knee reps and three controlled full push-ups. Omit eccentric-pushup. Replace wall-pushup with Push-up — 3 × 4–10, rest 120s, 2 RIR.
- **advanced:** Two sessions of 12 controlled full push-ups without sagging or shoulder discomfort. Omit eccentric-pushup. Replace wall-pushup with Archer Push-up — 3 × 3–6 / side, rest 120s, 2 RIR.

**Progression:** Begin in the wall stage and add controlled reps while keeping 3 RIR. After two sessions of 15 well-controlled wall reps per set, increase the lean or move to the knee/eccentric stage at 6–12 knee reps. Introduce the short eccentric sets only when you can lower without the hips sagging. Move to the full stage after two sessions of 12 controlled knee reps and three controlled full push-ups; keep at least 2 RIR while building 4–10 full reps. Use the advanced archer stage only after two sessions of 12 controlled full reps; start at 3–6 reps per side without twisting and return to the full variation if control declines. Add 5 seconds to planks up to 35 seconds before choosing a more demanding trunk position.

**Deload:** Weeks 4 and 8: halve working sets, rounding up, omit eccentric sets and use an easier push-up variation while keeping at least 4 RIR. Halve plank duration. Retain easy scapular practice and return to the last controlled stage after the deload; ability criteria still apply.

**Why this works:** A repeatable starting variation allows consistent practice while the trunk stays controlled. Small eccentric doses are introduced only after basic pushing ability is present. Later stages replace the easier movement instead of adding a second complete push-up workout.

**Volume notes:** This is skill practice, not an instruction to reach failure or maximize chest volume. The wall baseline has nine primary pushing sets plus small eccentric doses in the later introductory stage. Stage substitutions keep the weekly set count controlled.

- A solid wall and clear floor are sufficient. No bench or elevated furniture is assumed.
- Apply stage omissions and substitutions before the baseline session. Changing stage is a deliberate ability-based choice.

**Progression:** Use a bell that is manageable for the most demanding listed movement. Add controlled reps in the squat, deadlift and lunge ranges before using a heavier bell if available. For swings, add one rep to at most two sets per build week, up to 15 per set, only while every rep remains crisp; never shorten rest and add reps in the same week. Carries add 5 metres per side per build week up to 30 metres on a clear route. With one fixed bell, keep the set ceiling and progress reps or distance rather than inventing load increases. Use the kb-deadlift entry instead of swings until the hinge is controlled.

**Deload:** Weeks 4 and 8: halve working and swing sets, rounding up, reduce carry distance by half, and keep rep work at least 4 RIR. Use an easier bell if available; otherwise use the low end of the rep range and omit swings if hinge control is not fresh. Retain easy warm-up and full recovery between short sets.

**Why this works:** Controlled lifts and carries provide work with a single load while swing practice adds repeated hip extension. The sessions separate crisp ballistic sets from grinding strength efforts. Rest intervals and a fixed set ceiling make conditioning progression easier to monitor.

**Volume notes:** Swing and carry sets are conditioning or technical work with hardSet false; they are not counted as full hypertrophy sets. The plan does not promise balanced upper-body muscle growth from one kettlebell.

- The title describes an eight-week training block, not a seasonal deadline or a guaranteed appearance.
