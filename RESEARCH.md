# Program design and evidence

Evidence reviewed: **31 October 2026**.

The new starter templates are original implementations of published training principles. No study directly validates these exact app routines. Custom programs are authored by the user; the original Forge routine remains available for continuity and is labelled as a legacy plan.

## Evidence and how it informs the app

1. **Currier et al., ACSM position stand, 2026.** [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897), [ACSM summary](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/). This overview synthesizes 137 systematic reviews. The app offers routines that engage the main muscle groups across at least two weekly sessions. Goal-specific loading and volume matter; failure training and complicated programming are not prerequisites. Beginner templates start with two working sets, and the app uses rep ranges and effort guidance instead of prescribing a starting weight from body mass. Strength templates use lower main-lift rep ranges for experienced users. The precise rep ranges and exercise choices are implementation decisions, not direct quotations of a tested protocol.

2. **Ramos-Campo et al., 2024.** [Split versus full-body routines: systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/). With volume equated, neither split nor full-body routines showed a clear overall advantage for strength or muscle growth. The app therefore lets users select a schedule and structure they can sustain, rather than claiming a particular split is universally best.

3. **Pelland et al., online December 2025; journal issue February 2026.** [Weekly training volume and frequency: meta-regressions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/). Adaptations show dose-response relationships with diminishing returns, and direct versus indirect work matters. The advanced muscle-building variants provide additional sets on the first four exercises; the app encourages gradual adjustment based on recovery. It does not assume unlimited extra volume is better or assert that every program delivers an optimal number of sets for every individual.

4. **Refalo et al., 2025.** [Sex differences in muscle growth: systematic review and Bayesian meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40028215/). Relative hypertrophy potential was broadly similar across the studied male and female groups. These findings do not define gender-specific programs. The app asks optional gender and body-weight questions as requested, but bases suggestions on training goals, experience, equipment, and available days. It does not infer biological sex, health status, exercise readiness, or starting loads from gender identity or body weight.

## Recommendation rules

- Gym, 2 days: full-body A/B. Gym, 3 days: full-body A/B/C.
- Gym, 4 days: upper/lower repeated. Gym, 5 days: upper/lower plus a full-body session.
- Dumbbells and a bench: two- or three-session full-body programs. Requests for four or five days suggest three training sessions with an explanation, rather than silently adding more work.
- Beginner: two working sets and manageable loads. Intermediate: three working sets. Advanced muscle-building: four sets on the first four movements; three on the remainder.
- Strength focus: main movements use 6–10 reps for beginners and 4–6 for experienced users. Other goals mainly use 8–12 or 10–15 reps. These are practical starting ranges, not claims of a uniquely optimal prescription.
- Body-composition goals do not trigger calorie targets, predicted weight loss, or heavier starting loads. The app clearly explains this limit.
- Progression suggests the smallest available load increase only after all sets reach the top of the range with controlled form and reps remaining in reserve. It does not automatically increase weight.

Users can preview every session, swap exercises, adjust sets, and choose a custom program instead of accepting the suggestion. These general adult fitness templates do not replace assessment of an injury or a condition that affects exercise.

## Demonstrations and privacy

All 42 selectable exercises have a YouTube demonstration link; see [VIDEO_SOURCES.md](VIDEO_SOURCES.md). Demonstrations are technique references, not the scientific evidence used to design the routines. Link discovery was verified against publisher exercise pages or indexed YouTube metadata. Playback can still depend on the creator, region, browser, and internet connection.

YouTube is contacted only when a user opens a demonstration. The embedded player uses `youtube-nocookie.com`, supports inline playback, and is removed when closed. Gender, body weight, and workout results are never added to video URLs or sent to a recommendation service. Profile and workout information stays in local browser storage. Exported backups include the profile and should be kept private.
